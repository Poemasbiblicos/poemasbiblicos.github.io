# Ten Biblical Poems for Mother's Day



---

## I. The Well of Hagar
*Genesis 16:7–13*

In the wilderness of Shur, where silence bleeds<br>
into the parched mouth of the afternoon,<br>
she knelt beside a spring that no one sees—<br>
only the angel knew that broken tune.<br>

Her eyes had swallowed distances and stone,<br>
her womb a country mapped in exile's grief;<br>
yet God descended where she wept alone<br>
and named her waters more than mere relief.<br>

"You are the God who sees me," Hagar said,<br>
and in that naming, mother-strength was born—<br>
not in a palace hall, nor cedar-spread,<br>
but in the dust, between the thorn and horn.<br>

O mother who has wandered unobserved,<br>
lift up your face: you too have been perceived.<br>



---

## II. Hannah's Canticle Before the Dawn
*1 Samuel 2:1–10*

My horn is lifted, not in triumph's cry<br>
but in the quiet grammar of the glad—<br>
for she who long was empty watched the sky<br>
until the morning answered what she had.<br>

She gave him back, the child of all her prayers,<br>
returned him like a river to its source;<br>
and in that giving, something broke through air,<br>
a music older than the throne's discourse.<br>

The barren woman blooms with seven sons,<br>
the mighty stumble where they thought to stand;<br>
what looks like dust beneath the winter sun<br>
is merely wheat, awaiting the Beloved's hand.<br>

O mother who has poured her sorrow out,<br>
your silence was a prayer without a doubt.<br>

## III. Ruth at the Threshold
*Ruth 1:16–17*

Where you go, I will go—not from duty<br>
but from some older loyalty of bone;<br>
your people's roads have turned to me with beauty<br>
I could not name until I walked alone.<br>

Naomi, grief had salted all your fields,<br>
you called yourself the bitter-tasted one;<br>
yet Ruth came clinging, knowing that love yields<br>
its truest harvest under the strange sun.<br>

Two women on a road the world thought spent,<br>
one widowed twice—in husband and in hope—<br>
and yet the grain was golden where they went,<br>
and Boaz stooped to let the poor one cope.<br>

O mother-bond not drawn from common blood:<br>
some love is covenant, not flesh and flood.<br>

## IV. Proverbs 31: Portrait in Cedar and Linen
*Proverbs 31:10–31*

Before the city stirs she lifts the dark<br>
like a loom that turns the night to useful thread;<br>
her lamp is not a torch for any park<br>
but fire that feeds the living and the dead.<br>

Her hands move in the language of the field,<br>
she plants a vineyard with her earned-out wage;<br>
her arms are strong enough to neither yield<br>
to indolence, nor drown in grief's long cage.<br>

What charm dissolves, what beauty time will spend—<br>
but she who fears the Lord outlasts them both;<br>
the market knows her name, the city's end<br>
praises her by the city gate with oaths.<br>

Give her the fruit, the praise her decades made—<br>
let her name stand where light does not fade.<br>

## V. Mary at the Annunciation
*Luke 1:26–38*

Into the ordinary room of her,<br>
the wing-sound came before the word arrived;<br>
she was not in a temple, not in prayer—<br>
only a girl inside the afternoon's quiet hived.<br>

"Fear not," said Gabriel, and in those words<br>
a universe was folded like a scroll;<br>
she heard the impossible become as birds<br>
that settle on the undefended soul.<br>

"Let it be done to me," she said—so slight<br>
a sentence for so vast a consequence;<br>
yet in her yes, the Infinite took flight<br>
and wore the weight of human permanence.<br>

O every mother who has said: "I will"—<br>
you carry heaven's purpose, trembling, still.<br>

## VI. Elizabeth's Leap
*Luke 1:39–45*

When Mary crossed the hill-country at speed,<br>
she carried news that prophets longed to hear;<br>
and in the womb of Elizabeth, indeed,<br>
a voice rehearsed its cry six months too early.<br>

The old woman felt joy before it spoke—<br>
her child was language before he had a tongue;<br>
and in that house where two women invoked<br>
the mercy folded into what was young,<br>

a blessing broke like bread across the air:<br>
"Blessed among women, blessed is the fruit"—<br>
not said in temple-hall or cedar stair,<br>
but whispered through a mountain-road's pursuit.<br>

O mothers who have met in this: your joy<br>
is holy when it greets another's boy.<br>

## VII. At Cana's Edge
*John 2:1–11*

She noticed what the steward had not seen—<br>
the stone jars emptying, the feast's defeat;<br>
her eye for need was sharp and evergreen,<br>
she knew her son before the crowd could greet.<br>

"They have no wine," she said—three words, no more—<br>
and in those three she placed all mothers' art:<br>
to see the lack before it hits the floor,<br>
to carry others' thirst inside your heart.<br>

He turned to her and said, "My hour not yet"—<br>
yet something in her silence made him stay;<br>
the water heard the word and would not let<br>
itself remain as water that same day.<br>

O mother: your persistent, quiet plea<br>
can move the Word himself to finally see.<br>

## VIII. The Woman Who Searches
*Luke 15:8–10*

She lights a lamp against the indoor dark<br>
and sweeps the floor like someone reading braille;<br>
each inch of dust becomes a sacred mark,<br>
each shadow hides what silver cannot fail.<br>

The coin is small—one drachma, barely weight—<br>
yet she who lost it cannot sleep or rest;<br>
she will not name the hour as too late,<br>
she will not say the search is dispossessed.<br>

And when she finds it, cooling in the crack,<br>
she calls her neighbors with a voice that rings—<br>
"Rejoice with me, the lost one has come back!"<br>
and heaven counts it among precious things.<br>

O mother who has searched through years of night:<br>
your seeking is already close to light.<br>

## IX. Jochebed: The Ark of Reeds
*Exodus 2:1–10*

She wove the basket like she wove a prayer,<br>
sealing the tar against the river's tongue;<br>
what Pharaoh's law had stripped from all the air<br>
she placed among the reeds and held her lung.<br>

The Nile would be the altar and the risk,<br>
the current a theology of trust;<br>
she named her fear and let it go—so brisk<br>
the current took him where it knew it must.<br>

Miriam stood watch at a careful distance,<br>
as mothers learn to watch when they must leave;<br>
and Pharaoh's daughter, moved by God's persistence,<br>
pulled Moses out to weep, to breathe, to believe.<br>

O mother who has let go what she loves—<br>
your act of faith is held by heaven above.<br>

## X. The Mother of Sorrows
*John 19:25–27*

She did not turn away when others fled;<br>
she stood beneath the cross like a deep root<br>
that does not know a way to leave its bed<br>
even when the tree is offering no fruit.<br>

What sword had Simeon promised long ago<br>
had found its way through all the intervening years;<br>
she felt it enter through the chest below<br>
the place where love accumulates its fears.<br>

"Woman, behold your son"—and then to John:<br>
"Behold your mother"—grief and grace in one breath;<br>
in dying he was still a mother's son,<br>
in sorrowing she bore him past his death.<br>

O every mother standing at the cross:<br>
your presence is not silence—it is force.<br>
